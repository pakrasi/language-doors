I · Prism

One meaning.
Five shapes.

A sentence is not its words. It is a recipe: a function, a door, a turn of the dial, some glue. Pass the same recipe through five languages and the light bends differently each time.

II · The machine

Turn it.

Pick a meaning. Then move it in time, negate it, ask it, or swap the glue. Every language re-forms at once. Watch which pieces travel and which stay put: that motion is the grammar.

III · Rivers

Where the order bends.

Ribbons run from the English word order to each language's. Parallel ribbons mean the language keeps English order. Braids mean it does not. Count the crossings and you have a measure of how far you must travel from your first language to sound native.

IV · Where time lives

One door, nine turns.

Take the door GO and rotate it through past, present and future, simple, progressive and perfect. Each language changes a different part of the sentence to do it. Only the changed words light up. Some languages move a mountain; some flip one particle.

V · Coda

The recipe never changed.

Every sentence above was the same string of IDs. Function, door, turn, glue. What each language did with them is what you are learning. The reference table lists the parts; the drill makes you reach for them without looking; this page is for understanding why they land where they do.
